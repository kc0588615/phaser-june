## 2. Farms & towns

**Recommendation: remove it.** Of the 20 animals that answer yes, 11 do so on a link a player wouldn't call "lives on farms or in towns" (cut forest, man-made ponds and canals, or land IUCN itself rates marginal), and 6 animals that answer no have a game line or IUCN entry that puts them on farms, in a city or in canals. Nothing a player can see answers it except one generic field-guide line. Removing it loses no playable round (8 world seeds gain one) and touches about 9 code sites, 20 content files and the rules version. A stricter, reworded version is possible (option C) but keeps most of the problem. 18 findings below are about this clue: 13 `unfair`, 5 `unclear`.

### What it is and where it came from

- Column "Farms & towns", legend "Lives on farms or in towns?", question "Does it also live on farms, plantations or in towns?" ([`questionMatch.ts:152,171,200`](../../src/clueGame/questionMatch.ts)), gem picture 🏡 ([`clueFaces.ts:15`](../../src/clueGame/clueFaces.ts)). The yes-animals get one field-guide line: "It also lives on land people have changed, like farms, plantations or towns." ([`profiles.ts:93`](../../src/clueGame/profiles.ts)). Four wordings, each wider than the last.
- It is IUCN's habitat class 14/15, "Artificial/Terrestrial" plus "Artificial/Aquatic", copied into content with the rule "IUCN habitat classes and level-2 kinds, marginal ones included" (`docs/CONTENT_SOURCES.md:24`, plan 040). It was never designed as a question: it became askable when every `habitat:` tag did (plans 041–044). IUCN's "Artificial" covers arable land, pasture, plantations, rural gardens, urban areas, **heavily degraded former forest**, and man-made **ponds, canals, reservoirs and ditches**.
- The content rule says to skip Artificial/Aquatic rows, but three holders hold it only through them (2, 73, 74), and the purple frog (16) has the same kind of rows as the Goliath frog (11) yet answers no. The rule was applied unevenly.

- **Kind lines** ("It also lives in farm fields.", "…in towns or cities.", "…tropical forest that has been heavily cut.", "…ponds, canals or reservoirs people have made."; [`profiles.ts:139-145`](../../src/clueGame/profiles.ts)) would say *why* an animal holds it, but kinds aren't askable, so they never reach the field guide.
- **Witness notes:** two hand-written notes say it (13, 54, tag `town_dweller`), but a witness note shows only when two suspects share its tag, and no dealt set holds both.
- **Threat lines:** over 20 animals have lines like "forests are cleared for farms" ([content](../../db/content/animals/07-hairy-frog.json)), which correctly read as no, but put the label's words in front of the player all the time.

### How rounds use it

From dealing every seed the way the game does (rules `053-1`; the world and the four continents with 12+ animals; `out/s2-deals.md`):
- It is the **second most dealt question in the game**: in 36 of 141 seed decks (25.5%), after only "Large 30–300 kg" (39). World 26.5%, North America **100%** (13 of 13), South America 14%, Asia 10%, Africa 4%.
- In 28 dealt rounds it is the only one of the four clues that tells some pair of suspects apart, so a player who misreads it for one of those two can't separate them from the clues alone. Examples: red-eyed tree frog / coastal tailed frog, the two sloths (30 / 54), De Winton's golden mole / golden-rumped sengi, axolotl / hellbender, spiny softshell / hellbender.
- In 5 North American sets the two sloths (30 pygmy three-toed, 54 brown-throated) differ on nothing else in their whole record.
- **Without it:** no seed loses its round. 8 world seeds that have no playable set today gain one (68 → 76 of 76), because the greedy suspect picker no longer lets in a frog that only Farms & towns separates. 3 world sets, 1 African and 5 North American sets change (the two sloths no longer share a set); 31 more decks change their four questions.

### What removing it touches

Full list with line numbers in `out/s2-trace.md`.
- **Code (9 sites):** `traits.ts:27` (`HABITAT_CLASSES`, which `COMPLETE_PREFIXES` spreads); `questionMatch.ts:152,171,200` (question, legend, column); `clueFaces.ts:15` (🏡); `profiles.ts:93` (guide line) and `profiles.ts:139-145` (7 kind lines).
- **Trap:** `artificial` must leave `COMPLETE_PREFIXES` in the same change as the 45 `artificial:<kind>` entries leave the content and the live rows. Otherwise `isHandWrittenClue` (`profiles.ts:291`) stops seeing kind lines as generated, and "It also lives in towns or cities." turns up as a witness note.
- **Content:** `artificial` and its kinds out of 20 files' `habitats` (no animal is left without a habitat class). `npm run content -- check` fails loudly on any file still listing it.
- **Live rows:** 20 `habitat:artificial` and 45 kind rows in `species_deduction_clues` go with the owner's prod content build. `clue_match_solves` stores no clue tags, so past results need nothing.
- **Rules version:** bump `ANIMAL_RULES.version` (`animalBoard.ts:58`, now `053-1`), because decks change, so solve stats stay separable. Saved games would already restart safely (the resume check compares every saved row).
- **No tests, docs or site copy** name it. `docs/CONTENT_SOURCES.md:24,47` (the IUCN-class rule) should say the class is skipped. The balance bot's numbers in plans 044/047/053 were measured with it.

### Options

**A. Remove it (recommended).** The trade-offs:
- For: ends 18 findings at once; no round lost, 8 gained; small, mechanical change; nothing a player can see is lost, because the clue had no visible tell.
- Against: Habitat drops from 10 questions to 9, and North America loses the clue it deals in every round, so its decks shift to other questions (all 13 seeds still play). The farm-and-town threat lines stay, but they then mislead nobody, because there's no question to misread them against.

**B. Replace it with something a player can check.** Nothing in the current data is ready. The data support for each candidate, counted across the 76:
- *How it moves* (`travelOf`: fly, swim, dig, walk). It is already derived, for the cards' exit animation, but it isn't a fact: "walk" is the default when no tag says otherwise (43), and "swim" (14) puts the tiger, the Asian elephant and the jaguar beside the axolotl, and overlaps the Fresh water question (`system:freshwater`: 8 of the 14 hold it).
- *Lives in trees.* The best candidate: players can often see it, and it's natural to ask. The open tag `tree_dweller` is on 11 animals (5, 6, 23, 30, 35, 40, 43, 45, 54, 61, 71), and 9 of their photos show the animal in a tree (40 orangutan and 71 Andean bear are on the ground; `out/s8-tree-photos.png`). But the tags were written only where a note needed one: the golden snub-nosed monkey (34) and Sunda pangolin (24) are tagged only `climber`, `climber` also covers rock-climbing turtles (4, 15) and climbing jaguars and bears, and the chimpanzee has neither. A question treats a missing tag as no, so this needs a new trait researched for all 76, with a written rule (sleeps in trees? feeds in trees?) and a guide line. That is a content mission of its own, not a swap.
- *Burrows* (`burrower`, 18 animals): the same coverage problem (tagged only where a note needed it), and photos rarely show it.
- *A habitat kind* (plantations, pasture…): the same IUCN data, so the same problem, and kinds aren't checkable either.

**C. Keep it, reworded, with strict holders.** Make the four wordings one, e.g. column "Farms & towns", question "Does it live on farms, ranches or in towns?" and the guide line saying the same. Hold it only where IUCN rates farmland, pasture, gardens or towns Suitable, or its text says the animal lives there: the 9 "really" holders, plus the giant anteater if "plantations" stays in the question, plus the two non-holders IUCN puts on farms (56 chimpanzee, 63 Temminck's pangolin). That is 12 holders. 10 drop it (2, 6, 11, 23, 25, 28, 32, 59, 73, 74).
- For: answers become true in the plain sense; dealt in 39 of 149 seed decks (26%; world 27 of 76, North America 7 of 13), and no seed is lost (`audit/s8_strict.ts`, `out/s8-strict.json`).
- Against: still no visible tell (no photo shows it), so the player still depends on one guide line; it needs a written rule in `docs/CONTENT_SOURCES.md` and a judgment per animal; 16 content files change (10 drop it, 2 add it, 52 loses the `urban` kind IUCN doesn't support, and 18, 65 and 71's lines are reworded), against 20 for removal; and every future animal needs the same judgment. Without strict holders (rewording alone), the 11 marginal and water-only yeses stay.

**Line for section 1:** Remove Farms & towns. It is IUCN's "Artificial" habitat class (cut forest, canals and marginal land included), not something a player can check: 11 of its 20 yes-animals don't plainly live on farms or in towns, 6 no-animals have game lines or IUCN entries that say they do, and no photo shows it. Removing it loses no round. If the owner wants to keep it, option C (one wording, 12 strict holders) is the fair version.

## Method and coverage

Every check has a row in `findings.csv` (`/srv/critter-ai/agent-state/missions/clue-audit/`, with the audit scripts in `audit/` and their output in `out/`), passes included.

**Content and what players see (S1).** The 76 animal files (`db/content/animals/`) were loaded through the game's own code (`animalsFromProfiles` and `makeBook`), so the inventory (`out/inventory.md`) shows exactly what a round holds: each trait's value, note and source; systems and habitats; the askable answers; the field guide lines; witness notes (with tags), untagged notes, reveal-only notes and facts; open tags; the photo. A read-only comparison with the live database on 2026-11-06 found the files and the live rows identical: 76 species, 2,430 clue rows and 437 facts, 0 differences. The same 20 animals hold `habitat:artificial` live and in the files.

**Mechanical checks (S1, `audit/mechanical.py`).** 1,075 rows: each of the 76 animals × 14 prefixes at least once.
- Size, lifespan and young: the numbers in each note are parsed into the bins the column labels give (size: under 100 g, 0.1–2 kg, 2–30 kg, 30–300 kg, 300+ kg; lifespan: under 5, 5–20, 20+ years; young: 1–2, 3–10, 10+). One bin: pass. A range across an edge: straddle. A value on an edge, a bound only, a record or the wrong quantity (generation length, age at maturity, gestation), a length instead of a weight, or no number: flagged for the source check. All 189 parsed values were read by hand against their notes; the parser misreads none.
- Diet, activity, social, covering and birth: a keyword scan of each note against its value (for example an "Only plants" animal whose note names insects or eggs).
- Cross-checks: eggs against litter wording and live birth against clutch wording; systems against habitat classes (sea against Coasts & sea, fresh water against Wetlands, land against a land class); every habitat kind has its class; open tags against values (`town_dweller` without Farms & towns, `fruit_eater` with Only meat, `coastal_dunes` without Coasts & sea, and others); taxonomy (genus is the first word of the scientific name; one order per family and one class per order across the 76).
- Missing traits are `no-record` rows: size 9, lifespan 17, young 13, diet 1, activity 14, social 20; birth and covering are recorded for all 76.

S1 left 126 open flags (23 straddles, 81 note-value flags, 22 cross-check flags). Later steps resolve each against the cited sources.

**Farms & towns trace, deals and rules (S2).** Every read of `habitat:artificial` in code, content, tests, docs, the site and git history (`out/s2-trace.md`). Rounds were dealt as the game deals them (`audit/deals.ts`, mirroring `useAnimalSession`: the world and every continent with at least 12 animals, each animal as a seed, `pickSuspectSet` then `dealClues` with rules `053-1`), once as is and once with the tag removed from every animal (`out/s2-deals.md`): 141 seed decks across 5 places. The rules table (`out/s2-rules.md`, `out/s2-rules-table.md`, `audit/rules.ts`) sets each askable tag's column label, legend, question and field guide line beside the content rule, with value counts across the 76; label and rule problems are `rule` rows in `findings.csv`.

**IUCN habitats, systems and taxonomy (S3, `audit/fetch_iucn.py`, `parse_iucn.py`, `compare_iucn.py`, `s3_rows.py`).** Each animal's latest Red List assessment was read on 2026-11-06 through headless Chrome (the site refuses plain downloads): the species page for the latest assessment id, then its print page with the Appendix habitat table (class, kind, suitability, major importance), the Habitat and Ecology text and the taxonomy. All 76 loaded; each content assessment is still the latest except the African forest elephant's (IUCN published a 2026 one; same habitat classes). For the 9 turtles and tortoises the content's `iucnId` is the range shapefile's id, not the Red List taxon id in the cited link, so the cited link was used (the live range table confirms each id is the right species). Content classes and systems were compared with the IUCN table, and every class IUCN's table lacks was checked against IUCN's own text or ADW. Habitat kinds aren't askable, so kind gaps are listed (`out/s3-compare.md`) but change no answer. For the 20 Farms & towns holders, `out/s3-farms.md` has each one's IUCN Artificial rows and suitability, the IUCN and ADW text, every line the game shows, provenance from git history, and a look at its photo (`out/s3-holder-photos.png`). Rows: 76 habitat, 76 system, 304 taxonomy (class, order, family, genus), 20 holder verdicts and 5 non-holder findings.

**Size, lifespan and young against the sources (S4, `audit/fetch_s4.py`, `source_text.py`, `numbers.py`, `s4_rows.py`).** Each trait's cited page was downloaded on 2026-11-06 (ADW, Wikipedia through its API, Smithsonian, San Diego Zoo, TFTSG, AnAge, USGS, Queensland government, AWC, the news story; the 13 IUCN citations reuse S3's pages), plus the animal's ADW account as a second source when it cites something else. `audit/numbers.py` pulls every sentence with a weight, a span of years or a count of young into `out/s4-excerpts.md`; every one of the 228 animal × trait cells was then judged by hand (`audit/s4_judgments.psv`), and all 54 S1 flags on these traits were closed.
- Rules used. Size and young: a range with less than two thirds inside the held bin is a straddle, `unfair`; a smaller overlap passes, with the overlap noted. Young: when the typical litter sits on the 1–2/3–10 edge (an average of 2 to 3), it is `unfair`. Lifespan: the game's rule is the longest known, so the value is checked against that, but a note that leads with a typical or wild figure inside "5–20 years" on a `long` animal is `unfair`. A note that gives a different quantity (generation length, age at maturity, gestation) is `unclear`. Two sources that disagree are both reported (`unclear` or `unfair`), never settled.
- Couldn't load: AmphibiaWeb (curl, Chrome and WebFetch all time out from the server) for 3 cells: Darwin frog's and red-eyed tree frog's young were checked from a search excerpt of the AmphibiaWeb page and ADW, and Wallace's flying frog's size is `couldnt-check` (no source gives a weight). Britannica (403 to every route) for the two Galápagos tortoises: checked against Wikipedia instead. The two `literature` DOIs are paywalled: the Sunda pangolin's size was checked against Wikipedia and the giant pangolin's against ADW and Wikipedia.
- Traits the game leaves out (39 `no-record` cells): 7 are documented for the species by ADW and could be added (Blanding's turtle 3–19 eggs, coastal tailed frog 44–85, dyeing poison dart frog 5–10, pig-nosed turtle 22.5 kg, brown-throated sloth 30–40 years, giant otter 1–5 young, golden poison frog up to 5 years in zoos); two of those would land on an edge (Blanding's turtle's average of 10 eggs, the giant otter's usual 2–3). The other 32 aren't in the source, or only as a relative's figure.

**Diet, activity, social life, covering and birth against the sources (S5, `audit/behavior.py`, `s5_rows.py`).** S4's cached pages, read for these traits' sections (ADW food habits, behavior, reproduction and physical description; the whole IUCN, Wikipedia and other pages). `audit/behavior.py` pulls every sentence with a diet, activity, social, covering or birth word (nocturnal, diurnal, crepuscular, cathemeral; solitary, pairs, herds, colonies, families; herbivore, omnivore, insectivore, fruit, carrion, prey; eggs, clutch, gestation, litter; fur, shell, scales, skin, spines, armor) into `out/s5-excerpts.md`, sentences naming another value first. Every one of the 380 animal × trait cells has a `source-value` row: hand judgments (`audit/s5_judgments.psv`) for every disagreement and all 52 S1 flags on these traits; the rest quote the first source sentence naming the held value (each quote read by hand; 14 noisy ones replaced).
- Rules used. Diet: an "Only plants" animal whose cited source names animal food, or a meat animal the source says eats mostly small invertebrates, is `unfair` (the doc's diet rule has no "only", the column has); two sources that disagree are `unclear`. Activity: a note that names a second activity (the legend drops the question's "mainly") is `unclear`; a note whose emphasis points to another column is `unfair`. Social: a groups animal its source describes as a pair with young is `unfair`, because the Pairs question says "pairs or small families". Covering: a note that calls the coat by another column's name (the echidnas' fur, the armadillo's shell) is `unfair`; how much hair makes fur is `unclear` (no rule).
- Couldn't load: AmphibiaWeb for Darwin frog's activity (`couldnt-check`; ADW and IUCN say nothing), and Wallace's flying and purple frogs' covering (passed on their class: frogs have bare skin; ADW and IUCN only mention it). Britannica for the Galápagos tortoises' diet and birth and the spiny hill turtle's birth, and the purple frog's paywalled DOI: checked against Wikipedia.
- Traits the game leaves out (35 `no-record` cells): diet 1, activity 14, social 20. Two are documented and could be added: the Floreana and Pinta tortoises are active by day (Wikipedia). Blanding's turtle (ADW lists both day and night), the Andean bear (sources disagree) and Goodfellow's tree-kangaroo (solitary only at genus level) are fairer left out.

**Shown text against the record, animals 1–38 (S6, `audit/text_scan.py`, `s6_view.py`, `s6_rows.py`).** `audit/text_scan.py` scans every line the game shows for all 76 animals (field guide lines with their notes, witness notes and their tags, untagged notes, reveal-only notes, facts) against the animal's values, habitats, systems and open tags: S5's keyword lists for diet, activity, social life, covering and birth; weights, lifespans and counts of young against the bins; habitat words (forest, savanna, shrub, grassland, river and marsh, rock and cliff, cave, desert and dune, coast and sea, farm and town) against the classes it holds, and sea and river words against its systems (`out/s6-text-flags.csv`, 394 hits, 217 for animals 1–38). The scan only gives leads: each of the 38 animals was then read in full (`audit/s6_view.py`), every sentence against every answer it gives, the no's on the complete prefixes included. Each animal × prefix has a `text` row (532); the judgments are in `audit/s6_judgments.psv`, and every scan hit's resolution is in `out/s6-text-flags-resolved.csv`. The S1 cross flags about shown text or open tags were closed here (`fully_aquatic` with On land, `coastal_dunes` without Coasts & sea, `swimmer` without fresh water).
- Rules used. A shown line that says the animal lives in a habitat it answers no to is `unfair` (a careful player says yes); threats ("forest cleared for farms"), name and taxonomy words ("frogs", "family"), and what young, eggs or tadpoles do are not the adult's trait and pass. Rocks: living, resting or climbing among rocks reads as "Rocky places"; eggs, tadpoles or toe grip on stream-bed rocks doesn't. Swimming across rivers isn't living in fresh water. A note that already had a note-value or source-value finding gets no second one; its text row points to it.

**Shown text against the record, animals 39–76 (S7, same scripts).** The other 38 animals were read in full the same way, with the scan's 177 hits for them as leads. Each animal × prefix has a `text` row (532 more; 1,064 in all); judgments in `audit/s6_judgments.psv`, scan resolutions in `out/s7-text-flags-resolved.csv`. The three S1 `swimmer` flags here (Javan rhino, jaguar, brown-throated sloth) pass: no line says they live in fresh water.
- Rules added. Sheltering in caves reads as "Lives in caves?", as resting among rocks reads as Rocky places. Rocks under water (a hellbender's stream bed) aren't Rocky places for an animal that lives its whole life in the water. A place name with "coast" ("coastal forests of Kenya", "the Pacific coast of Colombia") is `unclear`, lighter than a note saying it lives near the coast. "Woodland" against a no on forests is `unclear`, as in S6.
